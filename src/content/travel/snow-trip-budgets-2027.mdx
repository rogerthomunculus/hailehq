### Smokies drive-to — _Budget_

Gatlinburg, Tennessee, with skiing at [Ober Mountain](https://obermountain.com/tickets). Load both cars and drive — no flights, no rental cars.

**Total ~$12,300** (likely $10–16k) · **~$6k per family** · –$24k vs. the Park City plan · ~9½–10 hr drive · elevation under 3,500 ft

**Best for:** a low-stakes test run. If you aren't sure the kids will like the cold, this answers it for a third of the price.

**What you get:** the cheapest option by far, with nothing non-refundable except the cabins; cheap lift tickets (last season $64 adults / $54 kids 6–11 on weekdays, [OnTheSnow](https://www.onthesnow.com/tennessee/ober-gatlinburg-ski-resort/lift-tickets)); an aerial tramway from downtown up to the ski area, plus tubing and an ice rink; easy fillers at Ripley's Aquarium, Anakeesta and Great Smoky Mountains National Park; big shared cabins, cheap in February.

**What you give up:** **snow you can count on** — February highs in town often reach the 50s, and Ober runs mostly on snowmaking; **scale** — 10 slopes and 4 lifts, a hill rather than a mountain; **the "wow"** — no dog sledding, no deep snow, no big mountain views; about 20 hours in the car round trip.

| Line                         | Estimate     | Notes                                                |
| ---------------------------- | ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Gas, two cars                | $600         | About 1,250 miles round trip                         |
| Lodging                      | $3,500       | One large cabin or two smaller ones, 7 nights        |

### Ogden Valley, Utah — _Best value_

A quiet farm valley about an hour north of Park City, based in Eden, with ski school at [Powder Mountain](https://www.powdermountain.com). Same flight, same Utah snow, half the lodging cost.

**Total ~$24,500** (likely $20–30k) · **~$12.3k per family** · –$11.5k vs. the Park City plan · Delta nonstop + 1 hr · sleep at ~5,000 ft

**Best for:** real Utah snow for the least money — the pick if $15k per family is too much.

**What you get:** the same once-daily Delta nonstop to Salt Lake City; group lessons that include the lift ticket at about $219/day/person (the adults' first-timer lesson, ages 13+, also includes rentals, so the 14-year-old joins the adults; kids 7–12 get an all-day lesson with lunch); suites with pools at WorldMark Wolf Creek, 5–10 minutes from the slopes; [Nordic Valley](https://www.nordicvalley.ski) next door for tubing and a gentler hill; Snowbasin, the 2002 Olympic downhill venue, for a gondola ride and lunch; lower altitude, easier on kids coming from sea level.

**What you give up:** **no dog sledding or sleigh dinner** in the valley (a Park City day trip adds about $1.4k for dog sledding); **little town life** — Eden has a handful of restaurants, Ogden's 25th Street is 25 minutes away; **driving for everything** — nothing is walkable, and the road up from Ogden is a canyon; Powder Mountain is a small, local-feeling resort, and some February weekends are passholders-only (Mon/Tue ski days are fine).

| Flights and bags             | $5,650       | Same as the Park City plan                                                |
| Lodging                      | $5,000       | Two 2-bedroom suites, 7 nights. $110–210/night in October; higher in Feb. |
| Two AWD SUVs                 | $1,500       | Same as Park City                                                         |
| Ski school, tickets, rentals | $3,750       | 8 people × 2 days at ~$219, plus kids' rentals                            |

| Insurance                    | $1,000       | Scales with prepaid cost                                                  |
| **Total**                    | **~$24,500** | Likely range $20–30k                                                      |

### Winter Park, Colorado — _Runner-up value_

A family resort about 95 miles from Denver, with condos in nearby Fraser. Cheaper flights and a full activity menu, but higher and harder to reach.

**Total ~$28,000** (likely $23–34k) · **~$14k per family** · –$8k vs. the Park City plan · Southwest nonstop + 2 hr · base elevation ~9,000 ft

**Best for:** a group that wants dog sleds and snowmobiles but needs to save money, and is comfortable with altitude.

**What you get:** three Southwest nonstops on weekdays (~2h50m, fares from ~$119 each way); kids' group lessons (ages 4–14) have included the lift ticket and lunch — confirm this season's offer on the [lessons page](https://www.winterparkresort.com/plan-your-trip/lessons); a full menu — tubing, dog sledding (2+), snowmobiling, Hot Sulphur Springs, skating; the Colorado Ski Passport ($76, grades K–6) covers Winter Park for the 10-year-olds.

**What you give up:** **altitude** — sleeping around 9,000 ft is the biggest risk on this list for kids coming from sea level; **Berthoud Pass** — the only drive in crosses a mountain pass that can close in storms; condos are ~3 miles from the resort, so you drive or shuttle to lessons; less town and fewer restaurants than Park City.

### Park City, trimmed — _Recommended_

The current Park City plan with four trims. Everything that makes Park City the right first trip stays.

**Total ~$30,000** (likely $26–35k) · **~$15k per family** · –$6k vs. the Park City plan · Delta nonstop + 40 min · sleep at ~6,500–7,000 ft

**Best for:** the easiest, most varied first snow week from New Orleans, at a price two families can agree on.

**The four trims:** stay at Canyons Village or Kimball Junction instead of near the Park City base, and ride the free bus to lessons (–$3k); keep dog sledding, drop either snowmobiling or the sleigh dinner (–$1.4k); cook breakfasts and a few dinners in the condos (–$500); buy gear at Costco or secondhand, and borrow from friends (–$700).

**What you give up:** walking to ski school — the bus ride, with four kids in ski boots, is the real cost here; one of the three big experiences; a few nights out.

### Park City, full plan — _Current plan_

The plan in the full **[Park City trip guide](/travel/park-city-2027/)**: two condos near the Park City Mountain base, every activity in the base itinerary. This is the benchmark the other options are measured against.

**Total ~$35,750** (likely $28–45.5k) · **~$18k per family** · ~$640/person/day · Delta nonstop + 40 min · sleep at ~7,000 ft

**Best for:** the easiest first snow week you can buy from New Orleans, with every extra. You pay for convenience and variety, not better skiing.

**What you get:** one nonstop flight and a 40-minute interstate drive — no connections, no mountain pass; walking distance to ski school, free buses everywhere else; group lessons for every age in the group; the biggest menu nearby — Olympic Park, dog sledding, snowmobiling, a sleigh dinner, tubing, the Alpine Coaster, a crater hot spring, and Main Street; a real town at night, so eight people aren't cooking in a condo every evening.

**What you give up:** about $6k more than the trimmed version, mostly for walk-to-the-slopes lodging; upper-mid-range for a family trip — $18k per family is what a nice international vacation costs.

### Vail, standard — _Context_

The most famous ski resort in the country, done the normal way: condos in Lionshead, group lessons, rental SUVs. Included to show what the name costs before any luxury.

**Total ~$44,100** (likely $36–56k) · **~$22k per family** · +$8k vs. the Park City plan · nonstop to Denver + 2 hr · base elevation ~8,150 ft

<Note type="warn" label="The catch">
  Vail costs more than Park City but is a worse fit for this group. Only about 18% of its terrain is
  beginner, and some of its green runs are narrow and steep for true first-timers
  ([Snowthere](https://www.snowthere.com/resorts/united-states/vail)). You'd be paying for a
  mountain you won't use.
</Note>

**What you get:** a beautiful European-style pedestrian village; kids' group lessons with lunch, up to 8 kids per group, based at Golden Peak ([Avant Ski](https://www.theavantski.com/vail-for-the-family)); Adventure Ridge at the top of the gondola for tubing, plus skating in Lionshead; bragging rights.

**What you give up:** **more money for less fit** — group lessons reported around $480/day off-peak and $600 at peak ([SkiTalk](https://www.skitalk.com/threads/vail-group-lessons-600-where-are-good-deals.38030/post-1138731)), lift tickets not included, peak walk-up tickets hit a record $356 last season ([SnowBrains](https://snowbrains.com/vail-resorts-peak-lift-ticket-prices-hit-record-356-with-six-mountains-now-overs-300/)); a longer trip in — two hours from Denver on I-70 over Vail Pass, which closes in storms; higher altitude than Park City.

| SUVs and parking             | $2,000       | Overnight parking in the village adds up                            |
| Ski school, tickets, rentals | $11,000      | Group lessons ~$500/day, early adult tickets, kids' $50 lift add-on |
| Activities                   | $5,000       | Tubing, dog sledding, snowmobiling, a sleigh dinner                 |

### Vail, luxury — _Context_

What a no-compromise week looks like: residences at the Four Seasons, a private instructor for each family, someone else handling the boots and the driving.

**Total ~$108,500** (likely $90–140k) · **~$55k per family** · ~$1,940/person/day · fly into Vail's airport

**Best for:** removing every bit of friction. Nobody drives, nobody carries skis, nobody waits in a lesson line. It doesn't make the snow or the kids' fun any better.

**What it looks like:** fly into Eagle County airport (EGE), 35 minutes from Vail, with one connection in first class and car service meeting you; two 3-bedroom residences at the Four Seasons Vail, from about $3,295/night each, one-week minimum, sleeping 7 ([Emerald Kite](https://emeraldkite.com/vacation-rentals/vail-lionshead-condo-876784/)), with ski valet; a private instructor per family for both ski days (Vail privates take 1–6 people, [Avant Ski](https://www.theavantski.com/vail-for-the-family), so eight people need two, and privates skip the lift lines); rentals fitted and delivered to the residence; private versions of the big experiences — your own dog-sled tour and snowmobile guide, and a sleigh-ride dinner such as Beano's Cabin at Beaver Creek; spa time for the adults, and nice dinners out every night.

**Why it's here:** as context — this is what "the best" costs, so the other options can be judged against it. Nearly half the budget (~$52k) is lodging alone, more than any other option's whole trip. On two beginner ski days, everyone is on the same gentle runs no matter what they paid. Lodging at this level usually needs big deposits and strict cancellation rules, which matters given the school make-up-day risk.

| Car service                       | $1,500        | Airport transfers and evening rides — the village is walkable                                               |
| Private lessons, tickets, rentals | $13,000       | 2 private instructors × 2 days at an estimated $1,500–2,000/day, plus 16 lift tickets and delivered rentals |

| Insurance                         | $6,000        | About 7% of the prepaid cost, with Cancel For Any Reason                                                    |
| **Total**                         | **~$108,500** | Likely range $90–140k                                                                                       |

<Section id="together" num="08" title="Deciding together" subtitle="A simple way for two families to land on a number without anyone feeling pushed.">

<div class="measure">

1. **Share the range before the plan.** Look at [the options above](#options) together and each family names a comfortable per-family number — it's easier to say a number than to push back on a plan someone is excited about.
2. **Split the core 50/50.** Flights, lodging, cars, ski school and insurance. Both families are four people, so even is fair.
3. **Make the extras opt-in per family.** Snowmobiling, the sleigh dinner and nice dinners out can be booked by each family separately, so either can skip them without changing the trip for the other.
4. **Agree on insurance and cancellation together.** The Mardi Gras dates are listed as possible school make-up days, which affects both families. Buy Cancel For Any Reason coverage within the window after the first deposit — see the [Park City guide's insurance section](/travel/park-city-2027/#insure).
5. **Book the first deposit by the end of October.** Lodging and ski school for early February fill up, and early-booking lift ticket prices end in early December.
